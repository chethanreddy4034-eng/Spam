📐 How Naive Bayes Classifies Emails

Naive Bayes calculates the probability that an email belongs to a class (\(C \in \{\text{Spam}, \text{Ham}\}\)) given the observed words (\(W = \{w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n\}\)).

1. Bayes' Theorem

P(Spam | W) = [ P(Spam) × ∏ P(wᵢ | Spam) ] / P(W)

Calculates the posterior probability given the product of individual word likelihoods multiplied by the prior probability of spam.

2. Laplace Smoothing

P(wᵢ | c) = (Count(wᵢ, c) + α) / (Total Words in c + α × |V|)

Prevents zero-probability errors when encountering words that never appeared in a specific training category (setting α = 1.0).

3. Log-Space Computation

log P(c | W) = log P(c) + ∑ log P(wᵢ | c)

Multiplication of many small probabilities causes floating-point underflow in computers. Adding log probabilities preserves numerical precision.

4. The "Naive" Assumption

P(w₁, w₂, ..., wₙ | c) = ∏ P(wᵢ | c)

Assumes that all words in an email are conditionally independent of each other given the class. Despite being simplistic, it delivers outstanding accuracy in spam filtering!
